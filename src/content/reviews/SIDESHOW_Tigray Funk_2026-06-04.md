## Year-end retrospective

So because this ended up edging out the top spot for my album of the year, I wanted to do a slightly deeper dive into why a rapper I'd never heard before this year ended up having my most listened to album of 2026.

There's a lot going on with this album at one time. On one end, you have a (from what I can tell fictional) discussion between a religious idealist and a young man. The older man appeals to what he believes is the inherent salvation in giving yourself over to God, to which the young man points out the ways in which colonialists have co-opted religion as a means to reinforce systems of white supremacy. The skit doesn't linger long, but this conversation of religion vs revolution did make me have to spin back, Googling quotes, thinking it was a snippet from a larger conversation.

Interlaced with this are Sideshow's raps diving into issues like addiction (the man loves his pharmacist), life being an immigrant in the US (originally hailing from the album's namesake Tigray in Ethiopia), being in an environment where he's constantly being pressed, and the idea of mentally always being ready for war. Now on the surface, you can find these exact same themes in just about any trap album released in the past decade or two, but there's a specific way in which he weaves this between surprisingly introspective tracks that makes it worth listening to what he's actually saying.

Songs like Volume Metric and Blame Burnays deal with the pain of loss, in particular the pain of his brother being killed and keeping a strong face through it. For me, I like when rappers occasionally peel the braggadocios mask back and this is a textbook example.

In between the 4 discs on this project is a story told in parts on the origins of predator and prey animals. And I won't pretend to say I fully understand the metaphor behind it and its relation to the album, but I do know that it didn't feel forced in, and was engrossing enough for me to get through the album once, then spend a good chunk of the year occasionally spinning it back.

Production wise, this entire project is spacey, shirking traditional 808s for more dreamy loops. That said though, it's clear there was thought put into the instrumentals, and on just production alone this thing has little to no skips.

For someone who I hadn't heard previously before this project started, this album sort of embodies one of things I wanted out of this listening experiement. Stepping out of your usual genres is an easy way to break out of your comfort zone, but even in places where I'd comfortably call myself a nerd, I wanted a surprise like this.
